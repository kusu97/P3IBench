THE IDEA

Understanding video
is just the beginning.

Recent multimodal large language models have advanced video understanding, but existing benchmarks mainly evaluate whether models can interpret observed visual content. In real-world scenarios, assistants must additionally anticipate what assistance is needed and decide when to provide it. To address this gap, we introduce P³IBench, a benchmark for proactive video understanding in real-life streaming scenarios. It evaluates three complementary capabilities: daily perception, risk prediction and intervention. We benchmark 12 representative open-source and proprietary video-language models, including streaming and adapted non-streaming systems. Our evaluation shows that current models struggle to make well-calibrated response decisions. Finally, we construct additional proactive training data and train P³I-Assistant-4B, achieving improvements on several tasks, though gains remain uneven across capabilities. Together, these findings show that effective proactive assistance requires not only visual understanding, but also precise response timing and appropriate restraint.

STREAMING SETUP

Models observe only past and current frames and decide when to respond.

Proactive Response Score (PRS)

0–100 · Higher is better

Both metrics follow the same three scoring steps. They differ only in how responses are matched to targets.

01

Score a response

Evaluate response oj against its matched target gi: correct content earns more when timing is close.

s(oj,gi)= c(y^j,yi)·e−0.05|tj−τi|

c: correctness of predicted content ŷj against reference yi—rules (0/1), or LLM-judged [0, 1] for intervention.
tj, τi: response and target times, in seconds.

02

Score a target

Take the best response score from Step 1, then divide by the number of matched responses to penalize repetition.

Si= maxoj∈Ois(oj,gi)|Oi|

Oi: all responses matched to target i, including zero-scoring replies.
No matched response: the target scores Si = 0.

03

Score the whole sample

Average all target scores from Step 2 and multiply by 100. The matching rule determines which PRS variant is reported.

PRS-U
Unidirectional

Match each response to the most recent target at or before its emission time.

PRS-U=100N∑i=1NSiU

A response emitted before the first target is assigned to that target with a score of 0. This response still counts in the denominator when computing the target score in Step 2.

PRS-B
Bidirectional

Match each response to the nearest target in time, allowing early responses to match future targets.

PRS-B=100N∑i=1NSiB

N is the number of ground-truth targets in the sample. SiU and SiB are the Step 2 target scores under the respective matching rules.
